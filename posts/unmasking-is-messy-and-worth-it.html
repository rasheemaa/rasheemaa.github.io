Mental wellness and unmasking

Unmasking Is Messy, Tender, and Worth It

August 22, 20265 min read
becoming
more me

Unmasking sounds like one brave movie moment where the music swells, you announce your truth, and suddenly every room feels easy. Real life is usually quieter. It can look like noticing that you have been uncomfortable for an hour and finally changing the light. It can look like saying, “I need a minute,” before your body has to scream it for you.

For a long time, many of us learn to measure safety by how little space we take up. We rehearse the right face, the right tone, the right amount of eye contact, the right response. We become excellent at reading everyone else while slowly losing the sound of our own needs.

You do not have to become a new person. You are allowed to become easier to hear.

Sometimes unmasking is almost invisible

It might be choosing the clothes that feel good instead of the ones that make the most sense to other people. It might be letting yourself move, rest, repeat a comforting song, ask a clarifying question, or leave before you are completely empty.

None of those choices need a courtroom defense. A need does not become more real after you prove it to everybody in the group chat.

Try curiosity before correction

When I notice the urge to immediately fix myself, I want to practice asking softer questions:

  • What is my body trying to tell me right now?
  • Would this feel different with less noise, less light, or more time?
  • Am I saying yes because I want to, or because no feels dangerous?
  • What would make this moment ten percent gentler?

Ten percent matters. Healing does not have to arrive in one glamorous package. Sometimes it is just enough room to breathe without performing the breath.

A permission slip for the awkward middle

Unmasking can change relationships. It can bring grief for the years spent working so hard to appear effortless. It can also bring relief, silliness, clearer boundaries, and the strange joy of realizing your real personality was never missing. She was simply waiting for safer conditions.

You are allowed to go slowly. You are allowed to keep some things private. You are allowed to experiment, change your mind, and learn what comfort feels like without turning the process into another assignment to ace.

There is no perfect unmasked version of you waiting at the finish line. There is only the growing practice of listening when you speak.
