I don’t want to get to the end of my life and realize I kept postponing the good parts until I felt better.

I keep imagining this future version of me who finally has everything together. Her mind is quieter. Her body cooperates. She knows what she’s doing, and she can enjoy a good day without wondering when something is going to ruin it.

I’ve been saving a lot of happiness for that girl.

Meanwhile, I’m the one here. Living the days. Getting older. Finding little things I love in a life I’m still figuring out.

In [my last edit](https://thesheemaedit.com/posts/who-am-i-when-the-distraction-ends.html), I wrote about what happens when the distraction ends. About the feelings that can still be waiting when the room gets quiet. I still know that feeling. Being able to laugh doesn’t mean I suddenly have nothing to cry about.

But I’ve been thinking about the moments when I *can* feel something good, and how quickly I sometimes talk myself out of them.

*How can you be happy when this is still happening?*

*What about the thing you haven’t figured out?*

*Don’t get too comfortable.*

A good moment barely gets started before I’m reminding myself why I shouldn’t trust it.

**Then I’m dealing with what hurts and interrupting what doesn’t.**

Choosing happiness, for me, means making room for something good without requiring everything else to be okay first.

I can’t decide that sadness is finished with me. I can’t make every day feel lighter by wanting it badly enough. But sometimes I do have a choice about whether I let myself stay in a moment that feels good.

Whether I enjoy the picture or inspect it for evidence that I look too happy to be struggling.

Whether I let myself look forward to something without adding, “If everything goes wrong, at least I expected it.”

I know that last one. Preparing for disappointment can feel responsible. Sometimes I’m just experiencing it early.

Some of the distractions I wrote about are also things I love. A conversation can give me a break and still mean something. Making something can occupy my mind and remind me that I have ideas I want to bring into the world.

The relief might be temporary. I still got to experience it.

And if sadness comes back later, I want to meet it without using it to declare the whole day a lie.

There are days when none of this feels available. Days when enjoying anything takes more energy than I have. I don’t need to turn those days into proof that I’m failing at happiness, either.

I’m allowed to need support. I’m allowed to rest. I’m allowed to have a hard day without being told I selected the wrong attitude.

But on the days when joy does reach me, I want to stop asking it to come back when my life makes more sense.

I want to wear something I love on an ordinary day. Make a plan because I’m excited about it. Let a good afternoon be a good afternoon, even with an unfinished life waiting around it.

I’ve spent so much time thinking about the person I’ll become when I’m finally okay.

**The person I am right now deserves happiness, too.**

*With Love, Sheema*
